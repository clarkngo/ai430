Example: Campus Help Desk Pipeline

A fictional, finished Term Project you can click through: an IT help agent with tools, memory and error handling, run through versioned data, a gated deploy, monitoring and a red-team suite.

⚠ Illustrative example only. Campus Help Desk Pipeline, its college, its team and its numbers are made up, and the demo is a scripted HTML model: no real model, database or network calls. Your project doesn't have to look like this. It has to be one end-to-end agentic pipeline operated with all four HOS.

🗂️

The project at a glance

Group of threeAll 4 HOS

The system. At a small, fictional college, two IT staff answer about 600 help requests a month from students and faculty: Wi-Fi, sign-in, laptop loans, software. Campus Help Desk Pipeline is an agent that answers from the IT knowledge base, remembers each person's devices, opens and routes tickets with a small classifier, and asks a separate asset agent about loaned laptops. The project is less the agent than how it's run: every change to its data, model or prompt goes through the same pipeline.

ActionWho decidesWhy
Answer from the knowledge baseAutonomousRead-only, and every answer is traced and judged.
Open and route a ticketAutonomousCheap to undo: staff can re-route it.
Extend a laptop loanAutonomous, limitUp to 7 days, own loan only; the asset agent checks both.
Reset multi-factor sign-inA person approvesAn attacker's favourite request. Always a hand-off.

    Three releases: what was in each

    Phase 1 · 2 · 3

    Campus Help Desk Pipeline didn't appear in Week 10. Each phase released a working system version and a report, the same as yours will.

    End of Week 3 · Phase 1

    System v0.3

    • Repo with a devcontainer, a README, a .gitignore and no keys committed
    • Ticket history under DVC with a schema, and the first routing model logged in MLflow
    • An ADK agent answering from the knowledge base in a Codespace
    • A one-page proposal: the system, which HOS goes where, and who owns what
    End of Week 7 · Phase 2

    System v0.7

    • ci.yml and a gated deploy.yml to a Hugging Face Space
    • Memory across sessions, and Langfuse traces with a judge score
    • No guardrails and no drift monitoring yet: listed honestly as known gaps
    • A 3–4 page progress report
    End of Week 10 · Phase 3

    System v1.0

    • Weekly ct.yml, the drift report and the alert that fired in Week 6
    • The retrieval benchmark, and the guardrails, A2A and red-team suite
    • One-click Codespace launch and a README with an AI tool disclosure
    • Final report, presentation and demo video
    What they cut, and said so

    Connecting to the college's real ticket system (v1.0 writes tickets to a local SQLite file) and a Spanish-language knowledge base. Both are on the "what didn't make it" slide. That's the Integration Debate in practice: they cut features, not the gate, the monitoring or the red-team suite.

    What makes this a strong example

    Use it as a checklist
    • It's a pipeline, not just an agent. The demo shows a change moving from a commit to a monitored deployment, not only a chat.
    • Every HOS is real, not a token mention. For each one the team can point to a file, a workflow run or a dashboard and say why it's that HOS (the litmus test in the second table).
    • The autonomy table came first. The guardrails and the red-team suite test against decisions the team wrote down, not against whatever the agent happens to do.
    • It shows real failures. A blocked prompt edit and a drift alert that fired for real are more convincing than a demo where nothing goes wrong.
    • Its numbers include the noise. The gate's margin comes from three-run spreads, and the judge's agreement with human grades is reported.
    • It's honest about what's open. The residual risk and the cut features are named, with what the team would do next.
    • Every member is visible. Owners per area, commits from all three, everyone speaks, and the title card names who built what.

    Now plan yours: map your pipeline, write your backlog, then use the freeze checklist and the Demo plan.
