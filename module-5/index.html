What Weeks 9–10 are for

20% of courseOutcomes CO1–CO6Solo or group

The Term Project is one end-to-end agentic pipeline: an agent that uses tools, keeps memory across sessions and handles errors, operated with the practices of all four HOS. Versioned data and tracked experiments (HOS 1), CI/CD/CT that tests, evaluates and deploys every change (HOS 2), observability, evaluation and retrieval monitoring (HOS 3), and red-teaming, guardrails and agent-to-agent communication (HOS 4). It also uses the judgment you practised in the Application Challenges: which actions the agent may take on its own, and when it should hand off to a person.

Pick a real system someone would run: a campus help desk, a clinic's appointment assistant, a small shop's order agent, a city service's FAQ agent. Whatever you choose, it needs at least one model or dataset that changes over time, so HOS 1 and the retraining job in HOS 2 have something real to do.

The project runs all term in three phases. By the end of Week 7 you have locked your scope (Phase 1) and have a rough working version (Phase 2). Phase 3 starts with the Week 8 architecture review, then a backlog in Week 9, then builds. Week 10 finishes, freezes, rehearses and presents.

Backlog and Sprint 1

Scrum-liteKeep it brief

Turn each box of your pipeline map and each missing piece into a user story ("As a [user], I want [capability], so that [benefit]") and tag each with the HOS it depends on. Then mark a small, realistic subset as Sprint 1: what you can actually finish this week. Stories are saved in this browser only; copy them into your PM tool.

Depends on:

Coverage

Sprint 1 should touch at least two HOS end to end this week, and all four by the Week 10 demo.

Litmus test for each HOS

For every HOS, can you point to a specific part of your project and say why it's really that HOS? "This is HOS 2: a prompt edit runs the evaluation in GitHub Actions and is blocked if quality drops", not "we use GitHub". If a HOS only has a token mention, the Architecture criterion will show it.

Build: same Create → Evaluate habit, bigger scope

Rest of Week 9 → Week 10
  • Start from your HOS starter repos. The pipeline, the workflows, the tracing and the guardrails are the skeleton. Change what your system needs; don't start over.
  • Scaffold with an AI assistant, then judge it. Ask for one story at a time, the way each HOS's Create stage worked, and read every diff. Never let it edit your tests or your red-team suite to make them pass.
  • Get one change through the whole pipeline first: edit a prompt, push, watch CI, the gate and the deploy, and see the result in Langfuse, before widening anything.
  • Measure before you tune. Three evaluation runs per change, so you know your system's run-to-run spread before you set the gate's margin.
  • Keep traces on. The demo needs one real failure from development: what broke, how it showed up, and what fixed it.
  • Watch the free tiers. A full evaluation or red-team run is many model calls. Use the scripts' pause options, and run the full suite on merge, not on every push.
  • Rehearse in the simulators when you're unsure how a layer should behave: 1 · 2 · 3 · 4.

Standups

Async is fine

Brief, regular check-ins once building starts; a group chat is fine. Solo? Write the same three lines to yourself, or post them to your instructor. The point is to catch "I've been stuck for three days" before demo week, not during it.

Standup — [date] — [name]
Did:      deploy.yml now blocks a candidate whose p95 is over 4 s (HOS 2)
Next:     first 15 red-team cases against the autonomy table (HOS 4)
Blocked:  the full eval hits the free-tier limit on every push — move it to merges only

Minimum bar

Outcome CO6

A project that meets the course outcome does all of this:

  • An agentic pipeline: an agent built with an industry-standard framework (Google ADK, as in the HOS) with at least three tools, memory that persists across sessions, and error handling (retries, timeouts, a hand-off to a person) shown working in a real trace.
  • HOS 1: data versioned with DVC, a validation schema that rejects a bad batch, and every training run and agent evaluation logged in MLflow, with a registry alias for what's in production.
  • HOS 2: GitHub Actions that test every push, gate every deploy to a free host on an evaluation, and run a scheduled retrain or re-evaluation.
  • HOS 3: traces with a judge score and latency for every answer, a judge checked against at least ten human grades, one alert with a threshold you can justify, and a retrieval benchmark if your agent uses RAG.
  • HOS 4: a red-team suite of 15+ cases with results before and after your guardrails, mapped to the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications; an autonomy table; one residual risk you name; and at least one A2A call that checks who is asking.
  • A README someone could read in 3 minutes: the problem, the pipeline schematic, the metrics, one failure and how it was fixed, an AI tool disclosure, and a link to the demo video.
  • It runs from a fresh Codespace with the commands in the README, and no keys in the repo.

Demo plan — show every HOS working

Presentation · 20 pts

A suggested shape for the live-demo part of your 15-minute presentation (the rest is the problem, the pipeline schematic and the results on your 15 content slides). Adjust the times to your slot; keep the order.

~0:30The system and its pipeline. Your schematic: where data and prompts change, and every stage a change passes through.
~1:30One request, end to end. A live request through the agent: its tools, its memory, the model it calls, and the trace it leaves (HOS 1 and 3).
~1:30One change through the pipeline. A prompt or data change: CI, the gate's numbers, the deploy, and what monitoring shows afterwards (HOS 2).
~1:00One real failure from development. What broke, how a signal showed it, and what fixed it.
~1:00The attack that fails, and the one that might not. One blocked attack with the layer that stopped it, and your named residual risk (HOS 4).
restQuestions. Expect "show me where HOS 2 is": have the workflow run with the gate's output ready.

Self-check and reflection

Feeds your Author Contribution statement

Self-check: can you explain, without notes, how each of the 4 HOS shows up in your final system; where tool use, memory and error handling live in the agent; and what your gate checks, with its numbers? Say it out loud once before the demo.

Reflection prompts

  • Each member: what you personally built (link commits or issues), one decision you argued for, and one thing a teammate did that made the project better. Solo: what you built, one decision you'd make differently, and one piece of feedback (instructor or classmate) that changed the project.
  • Everyone: one moment where the pipeline, a trace or the red-team suite caught a problem before it reached the demo.

Your final report ends with an Author Contribution statement detailing each member's weekly tasks (solo: your own weekly tasks); these notes are its raw material. Full description: the Term Project section of the syllabus (in Brightspace).
