Application Challenge 3: Harbor City Library Catalog Assistant

Take the monitoring from HOS 3 to a system you haven't seen: build the signals for a library's catalog assistant from a week of recorded traces, decide which one should wake someone up, and find out whether the decline came from the model, the retrieval, or the documents.

📏

Ground rules

Everything here runs in GitHub Codespaces on free tiers, with no credit card. Everything runs on the recorded logs in logs/, so the provided tests need no API key. The judge runs once over the week with your free Gemini key.
🏢

The scenario

Harbor City Public Library (fictional) has a catalog assistant on its website: patrons ask "do you have the new Ruth Ozeki book?" or "how do I renew a laptop loan?", and it answers from the catalog and the library's policy pages. Patrons say its answers have gotten worse over the last week. Nobody knows since when, or why: the library had no monitoring.

You get a week of recorded traces (logs/traces.jsonl: question, retrieved chunks, answer, per-span timings), retrieval logs, the policy pages from before and after a website migration on Wednesday, and 20 answers graded by a librarian (logs/librarian_grades.csv).

📐

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test in tests/test_monitoring.py.

PartMust doMust not do
monitoring/drift.pyCompare each day's questions with the first two days' (length, topic mix) and report which drifted.Compare against the same day.
monitoring/judge.pyScore each answer against rubric.md and report agreement with the librarian's 20 grades.Report judge scores without agreement.
monitoring/retrieval.pyReport context precision and recall per day against logs/known_passages.json, and retrieval ms.Score retrieval by the final answer.
monitoring/latency.pyReport p50 and p95 per day, overall and per span name.Report only averages.
REPORT.mdOne table, one row per day, with every signal.Leave out a day because its numbers look odd.

The open decision: which signal triggers an alert

The library has one part-time systems librarian. Her instruction: "Wake me up only if patrons are getting wrong answers. I'll look at a dashboard once a week." Which one signal should page her, at what level, measured over what window? Everything else goes on the weekly dashboard.

There's no single right answer, and no provided test for this case. You decide, implement it, prove it with your own tests, and defend it in DECISIONS.md.

🗂️

Where the decline came from

Patrons blame "the AI". Using only your signals and the logs, decide whether the decline came from the model, the retrieval, or the documents, or a combination, and when it started. Point to the specific numbers and at least two traces that show it. "The judge scores dropped" isn't a cause; it's a symptom.

🪜

Steps & what to submit

1Open it and run the tests

Open your own AC 3 repo (Planned the AC 3 invite (the Classroom50 link is added here once the AI430-AC03 template repo is built and tested)) in GitHub Codespaces and run pytest. The monitoring functions raise NotImplementedError.

2Build the monitoring

Fill in monitoring/ until pytest passes. You may use an AI assistant; tell it to work in this existing workspace and keep MODEL = "gemini-3.8-flash". Run the judge once over the week: python -m monitoring.judge --pause 4.

3Write your alert

Implement your alert in monitoring/alert.py, with at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py: the day it should first fire, and a normal day it must stay quiet.

4Fill in the report

Run python -m monitoring.report to write the table into REPORT.md, then add your notes under it.

5Write the memo

Fill in DECISIONS.md, about one page: your alert and its level, where the decline came from with the evidence, and what your AI assistant wrote, got wrong, or missed.

Submit (via Brightspace)
  • monitoring/ and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, pasted as text (all tests passing, including yours)
  • REPORT.md
  • DECISIONS.md
📊

How it's graded

Each criterion is graded on one part of your submission, at one of three levels.

CriterionWeightGraded onFullPartialMissing
Application10%REPORT.mdEvery signal for every day, from the library's own logs, with the judge's agreement shown.Most signals, but one is missing or computed on the wrong data.It restates the HOS 3 Salish Fresh signals, or doesn't run.
Analysis20%DECISIONS.md, section 2 (cause)You separate model, retrieval and documents with numbers and traces, and date the start.A cause with some evidence, but you don't rule out the others.No breakdown.
Evaluation30%DECISIONS.md, section 1 (alert)One alert, its level and window, judged against the librarian's instruction and false alarms, with an alternative you rejected.An alert with a reason, but no level, window or rejected alternative.No alert, or no justification.
Design40%monitoring/, your tests and the pytest outputAll provided tests pass, and your own tests check when your alert fires and when it doesn't.Most provided tests pass, or your tests cover only the easy case.It doesn't run, or you wrote no tests of your own.

How this differs from HOS 3's Understand & Refine: there you extended code you already understood, by hand, with no AI. Here the business, the code, and the decisions are new, AI help is allowed (say what it wrote in DECISIONS.md), and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make, not the typing.

🧯

Common snags

  • test_p95_per_span fails. You computed p95 over every span together. Group by span name first.
  • Agreement comes out as 100%. You compared the judge with itself. The librarian's grades are in logs/librarian_grades.csv.
  • Precision is 0 every day after Wednesday. That may be the finding, not a bug: check whether the known passages' ids changed in the migration.
  • 429 during the judge run. Use --pause 4; it's 200 answers on the free tier.
