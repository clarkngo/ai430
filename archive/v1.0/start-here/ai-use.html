AI Use Guide

You'll use AI assistants a lot in this course, and in a few places not at all. This page shows which is which, and how to say what you used.

About 5 minutesApplies to every module
🤖

The short version

  1. AI can help you build and think. In most of this course, using it well is part of the skill.
  2. You answer for what you submit. If it's in your work, you should be able to explain it and say whether it's right.
  3. Say what you used. Disclosing AI use doesn't cost you points.

The reason is the job itself. MLOps and AgentOps engineers spend their time deciding whether a pipeline, a model or an agent can be trusted in production, and you can't judge work you couldn't do or explain yourself. That's why every HOS starts with AI and ends by hand.

Keep your keys out of it

Never paste an API key, a token or your .env file into an AI chat, and never let an assistant write a key into a workflow file.

What's allowed in each activity

Activity and AI ruleWhat that means
HOS · Create / Scaffold
Expected		Use an AI assistant to build the pipeline, workflow or guardrail. Paste your prompt into NOTES.md.
HOS · Evaluate
Your judgment		An AI can suggest tests, but you run them and report what actually happened, including what the AI got wrong or missed.
HOS · Analyze
Your own words		You can ask an AI to explain code or a log while you learn it. The walkthrough you submit is your own explanation, not a paraphrase of AI output.

No AI		Done by hand. Every lesson labels this part "No AI assistant for this part."
Application Challenge
Allowed, disclose		Say in DECISIONS.md what your assistant wrote, got wrong or missed. The decisions and the reasons for them are yours to defend.
Muddiest Point
Think, don't write		Ask an AI first if you like, then write about what's still unclear, in your own words. If you asked, say so under "What you already tried."
Concept Debate
Think, don't write		Use an AI to test your thinking. Posts are your own words and your own reasoning. If AI changed your thinking, say how in one line.
Knowledge Check
No AI		Each quiz opens with a no-AI pledge. Retakes and hints are there so you can learn from your misses.
Term Project · System
Allowed, disclose		List every model, framework and coding assistant in your README's AI Tool Disclosure.
Term Project · Reports and presentation
Allowed, disclose		The analysis must be yours, and so must the words. Check every source and claim an AI gives you, because you answer for them. List the tools in your README.

How to say what you used

One or two plain sentences are enough: which tool, what you asked for, and what you did with the answer.

Not useful"I used AI for this assignment."
Useful"Copilot Chat wrote the first version of gates/check.py. It compared the candidate with the newest run instead of the champion model, so I changed it to load the alias and added a test for the first deploy."

How your work is checked

  • No AI detectors. They're unreliable, so your grade won't be based on one.
  • A short follow-up instead. Your instructor may ask you to explain a line of your workflow, change a threshold, or say more about a post. If it's your work, that takes a minute or two.
  • If that doesn't resolve it, your instructor follows CityU's Academic Integrity Policy (University Catalog → Student Rights and Responsibilities).

Not sure whether something is allowed? Ask before you submit.
